# Shared-Basis + Low-Rank-Delta Compression of Routed MoE Experts (literature state, Oct 2026)

> **Status (2026-10-21):** ACTIVE evidence base (W4/W8). Constants per the W4 recompute (G = 1.24 GiB/token); REAP-288 quant correction S5-F23 (Q4_K_M does not reduce G).

Operating-point reference used throughout: Qwen3-Next-class shapes (hidden d≈2048, per-expert SwiGLU intermediate f≈512, 48 MoE layers, 512 routed experts, top-10) give per-expert projections of ~1.05M params (~1 MB at Q8, 0.5 MB at Q4); 48 layers x 10 draws x 3 projections at Q8 ≈ 1.4 GiB streamed per token, matching the PS5 operating point. A rank-r delta per projection adds r·(d+f)·2B ≈ 5.1·r KB of streamed bytes and ≈ 5.1k·r FLOPs of compute per matrix (see Q5).

## Q1. MEET ("Mixture of Expert-Experts") and successors

### Takeaway
No published paper named "MEET" / "Mixture of Expert-Experts" could be found despite four targeted searches — the name and its "2.6x at <1% loss" claim remain unverified. But the mechanism MEET is said to use (shared expert bank + per-expert low-rank residual) is a real, fast-maturing compression family ("weight reconstruction" / "residual sparsification" / "basis-experts") with 7+ independent 2025–2026 publications. Strongest published results: SLBF (Oct 2026) keeps a 122B Qwen3.5 MoE within measurement noise of the original at 34% total-parameter reduction, and a 30B Qwen3-30B-A3B within −0.6 avg-points at 24% reduction. Nothing exists at 512-expert scale or on Qwen3-Next-class models; largest/most-granular models tested are Qwen3-30B-A3B-2507 (128 experts, top-8) and Qwen3.5-122B-A10B.

### Cited Findings
- "MEET / Mixture of Expert-Experts": no arXiv/HF/GitHub hit under the name or the 2.6x-at-<1% claim in four varied searches — unverified; do not cite.
- LightMoE (arXiv 2603.12645, Mar 2026): "expert replacing" — groups low-importance experts, reconstructs each as W_share + B_n'A_n' (shared full-rank base + per-expert LoRA, r ≪ min(n,m)); at 50% compression beats prior methods by 5.6% avg; needs brief LoRA fine-tuning to recover — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.12645)
- ResMoE (KDD '25, arXiv 2503.06881, Mar 2025): Wasserstein-barycenter "common expert" + compressed residuals (pruned or SVD low-rank), restored on the fly at inference; up to 75% parameter reduction per expert with "comparable performance" on Switch-8B, Mixtral-8x7B, DeepSeek-MoE; one-shot, data-agnostic, no retraining — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06881)
- D2-MoE / "Delta Decompression" (ICML 2025, arXiv 2502.17298): W_i = W_b + ΔW_i; W_b = Fisher-weighted merge of experts, further structurally pruned; ΔW_i compressed by truncation-aware SVD (deltas shown to be strongly low-rank); 40–60% parameter compression with >13% average performance gain over other compressors on Mixtral-8x7B, Phi-3.5-MoE, DeepSeek-MoE-A16B, Qwen2-MoE — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.17298), [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/gu25c.html)
- MoBE / Mixture-of-Basis-Experts (arXiv 2508.05257, published 2026 per SLBF's citation "Chen et al., 2026"): each expert's up/gate matrix W = A·Φ(Σ_j w_ij·B_j) with per-expert A and per-expert mixing weights over m shared basis matrices B_j — a shared-basis bank trained by data-free weight reconstruction — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.05257)
- SLBF / Shared Low-rank Basis Factorization (arXiv 2610.09342, 7 Oct 2026): improves MoBE by making each shared basis rank-k (U_jV_j), enabling m=n bases; data-free, no fine-tuning; preserves all experts and routing. Measured (9-benchmark avg): Qwen3-30B-A3B-2507 84.9 → 84.3 at 24% total-parameter reduction, 82.4 at 36%; Qwen3.5-122B-A10B 86.3 → 88.0 at 34% reduction (at or above original); Gemma4-26B-A4B-it 85.1 → 81.8 at 37%; Moonlight-16B-A3B 64.3 → 62.5 at 15% — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.09342v1)
- Residual Sparsification via Output Importance (arXiv 2609.00575, Sep 2026): formalizes the family — "decomposes each projection matrix of an expert into a shared base matrix and per-expert residual matrix, then compresses the residuals" — and analyzes why routing-aware residual compression matters; a successor to ResMoE/D2-MoE — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.00575)
- Related family members with numbers: MoE-SVD (ICML 2025): 60% compression, 1.5x faster inference, "minimal performance loss" — [OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=acJ3vdFljk). Sub-MoE (AAAI 2026): shared U-matrix via joint SVD of expert stacks + merged V; keeps 96%/86% of Mixtral-8x7B performance at 25%/50% expert reduction — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.23266). MoE-I² (EMNLP Findings 2024): 25% expert pruning + ~40% intra-expert low-rank → >50% expert-parameter reduction "performance largely preserved" on Qwen1.5-MoE-A2.7B, DeepSeek-V2-Lite, Mixtral — [ACL](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-emnlp.612). RS-MoE: low-rank + sparse decomposition of expert weights — [OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=5SQbPbCU6P). RFID-MoE (Feb 2026): SVD with frequency/information-density-adaptive ranks; explicitly reconstructs the compression residual "using a parameter-efficient mechanism" — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.09316)
- Practitioner system (unofficial, low-traction repo): RAVE "Router-Aware Virtual Experts" — shared centroids + rank-4/8 low-rank residuals on a 128-expert Qwen3-MoE: claims quality parity with 50% expert reduction, 52% memory reduction (57→27.6 GB), 86% of decode speed (11 vs 13 tok/s) — [GitHub](https://github.com/ulamai/rave) (3 stars; treat as preliminary anecdote, not peer-reviewed)

### Inferences
- PS5 packer view: the SLBF/MoBE recipe is the closest published analog of the proposal — PC-side packer solves a data-free per-layer reconstruction problem (shared bases U_jV_j resident in RAM; per-expert A_i + mixing scalars streamed, a few hundred KB per expert at most); routing and expert count are untouched, so the router weights stay as-is. A packer could equivalently implement the simpler D2-MoE/LightMoE form (W_e = W_base + B_eA_e) which is easier to express as two matvecs.
- For streaming (not storage), the relevant quantity is delta bytes per expert-draw, and the family's published "25–36% total-parameter reduction" understates streaming potential: the basis is resident and pays no bandwidth, so streaming reduction = full-expert bytes / delta bytes even when storage compression looks modest. But all published evaluations recompose W on the fly without separating resident vs streamed traffic — none quantified a "stream the delta" system.
- The 2.6x/<1% MEET claim is plausible in magnitude (matches D2-MoE's 40–60% compression at small loss and SLBF's 24% at −0.6), but until the paper is located it should not be relied on.

### Gaps
- MEET itself (origin, mechanism, the 2.6x claim, any 2026 follow-ups) — not found in searches; possibly a misremembered name (closest published twins: D2-MoE, ResMoE, MoBE, LightMoE).
- No basis/delta result on any 512-expert or Qwen3-Next/Flash-Next-class model; Qwen3.5-122B-A10B (86.3→88.0 at 34%) is the frontier data point and is the right scale proxy.
- No agentic-benchmark (tau-bench/SWE-bench style) results for this family; all quality deltas are perplexity/zero-shot/5-shot reasoning suites.

## Q2. MoE-LoRA-style basis decomposition, SVD-of-expert-stacks, and inference-path compatibility

### Takeaway
The "K shared bases + per-expert low-rank adapter" idea exists in two distinct literatures: (a) PEFT MoE-LoRA variants (CoMoL, HydraLoRA, etc.) where bases are trained, and (b) pre-trained-expert compression (D2-MoE, MoBE, SLBF, LightMoE, MoLE, Sub-MoE) where bases are extracted from existing weights. Type (b) is what matters here, and it is inference-path compatible in the exact sense required: reconstruction is "resident basis matvec + streamed low-rank delta matvecs" with the original router untouched and expert count preserved; Sub-MoE-style shared-U decompositions are literally one big matmul + skinny corrections. Published ratios for pre-trained experts cluster at 1.4–2.5x storage compression (≡ 40–60% reduction) at 1–3% loss on ≤16B models, degrading fast beyond.

### Cited Findings
- CoMoL (ACL Findings 2026): MoE-LoRA experts stored as per-expert core matrices M_i over bases U_B, V_A shared across experts; token routing preserved in core space; parameter count "comparable to standard LoRA" — PEFT-side evidence that expert-specific behavior collapses onto shared bases cleanly — [ACL](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-acl.811.pdf)
- HydraLoRA (NeurIPS 2024, cited in MoA, ACL 2026): asymmetric MoE-LoRA with one shared matrix — same shared-basis pattern in PEFT — [ACL 2026 MoA paper](https://aclanthology.org/anthology-files/pdf/acl/2026.acl-long.965.pdf)
- MoLE / "Mixture of Latent Experts" (arXiv 2503.23100, Mar 2025): re-parameterizes each expert as (shared projection into compressed latent space) then (expert-specific small transform); transforms pretrained MoEs via low-rank approx; at low-rank ratio r=0.8 the converted model is "competitive with the original" — this is a *shared-basis + small per-expert* architecture, mathematically the proposal's structure — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.23100v1)
- D2-MoE, LightMoE, ResMoE, MoBE, SLBF, Sub-MoE, MoE-SVD, RS-MoE: see Q1 — all preserve routing and expert identities by construction (weights are recomposed per expert; the router matrix W_g is never touched). D2-MoE explicitly states its result is "structured and acceleratable, requiring no extra training"; Sub-MoE's merged representation is W_merged = U·Σ·V_merged^T — a resident shared U times per-expert V (two-matmul form) — [D2-MoE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.17298); [Sub-MoE](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/download/39464/43425)
- Unified taxonomy (TMLR 2025 "Towards Efficient MoE: A Holistic Study of Compression"): splits MoE compression into Expert Slimming vs Expert Trimming; finds low-rank/low-similarity constraints — merging-only fails on specialized experts; quantization preserves more per byte than structural trimming at same budget — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.02500v1)
- Holistic study & Demystifying compression (arXiv 2406.02500): shared experts in DeepSeek/Qwen2-style models are disproportionately important and should not be pruned/compressed — a caution for treating the shared expert as a free basis source — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.02500)
- No results found for "SoRA", "MoDe", AdaLoRA-per-expert compression, "Mixture of Cluster-Conditional LoRA", or "single-stage anti-pruning" as basis+delta expert compressors (see Gaps).

### Inferences
- Inference-path compatibility is satisfied by every type-(b) method: expert output = (x @ W_base) + Σ_i (x @ U_i) @ V_i (+ per-expert A_i matvec for MoBE/SLBF) — 2–4 skinny matvecs alongside the resident-basis matmul; no retraining of the router, no change to which experts exist. This is exactly the "two cheap matvecs" property the proposal needs.
- PS5 packer view: packer emits (per layer) resident W_base + {U_j,V_j} (RAM, a few tens of MB per layer) and (per expert) {A_e, w_e} or (B_e, A_e) chunks (SSD, 8–64 KB each). The GPU kernel does: y = x@B_res + Σ_r (x@U_r)@(delta V terms); all resident data read once per layer regardless of which 10 of 512 experts are picked — this is the entire bandwidth win.
- Published quality: at the 4–8x *storage* regime the proposal wants, this family has NOT demonstrated <1–3%; its demonstrated zone is ~1.4–2.5x storage at ≤3%. The streaming proposal can still win at 4–8x streamed-byte reduction if residual quality after a strong resident basis holds — but that is precisely the untested claim (Q3 gap).

### Gaps
- "SoRA", "MoDe", "anti-pruning", "Mixture of Cluster-Conditional LoRA" as named MoE basis+delta compressors: no reliable sources found; SoRA in literature means sparse-LoRA training (PEFT), not expert compression.
- No paper reports basis+delta applied to a hybrid-attention (Gated-DeltaNet-style) model like Qwen3-Next; all methods assume SwiGLU-FFN experts.

## Q3. Has anyone done basis/delta specifically to cut SSD/offload STREAMING traffic?

### Takeaway
Closest published work: "Bandwidth-Efficient Adaptive MoE via Low-Rank Compensation" (arXiv 2512.17073, Dec 2025) — precomputed per-expert low-rank compensators streamed *with* the top-n experts during offloaded inference, 3–8x end-to-end speedups — which validates low-rank factors as bandwidth currency for MoE offloading. But the exact proposal (one big shared basis permanently RAM-resident, per-expert *delta only* streamed, expert recomposed on the fly) has not been published as a system as of Oct 2026: the offloading systems literature (ktransformers, HybriMoE, MoE-Lightning, DALI, BlobPager-lineage, FluxMoE) moves whole quantized experts and does no basis/delta decomposition; the basis/delta papers (SLBF, MoBE, D2-MoE) are storage-compression papers that never model a streaming hierarchy. The combination is open and would be novel.

### Cited Findings
- Bandwidth-Efficient Adaptive MoE via Low-Rank Compensation (arXiv 2512.17073, 18 Dec 2025, RPI/UMass/Samsung): quantize-then-compensate for offloading. Offline: kurtosis-guided rank allocation per expert (buckets 0/16/32/128/.../1024 under an average-rank budget), HQQ-optimized low-bit weights + truncated SVD of the quantization residual, INT3-quantized U,V stored. Inference: stream the low-bit expert + its rank-r factors only for the top-n (n<k) router-ranked experts; on-device reconstruct W_hat = Q^-1(Q(W)) + U·V. Results: on Mixtral-8x7B, INT2 rank-16 compensation transfers 0.32 MB/expert (0.75% of the INT2 expert) and recovers most accuracy (+8.24 avg pts, +10.5 MMLU vs HQQ INT2); GPU-only offload throughput 2.37 → 18.11 tok/s (INT2) vs Mixtral-Offloading baseline; vs Hobbit 6.75 → 36.97 tok/s; GPU-NDP (MoNDE) 11.56 → 77.33 tok/s; 3–8x across systems. Their roofline measurement: offloaded MoE decode is I/O-bound, expert compute is a small share of runtime — the same idle-GPU regime as PS5 streaming — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.17073v1)
- MoE-Compression (arXiv 2509.07727, Sep 2025): uses error-bounded *lossy compressors* (SZ3, CuSZp) on non-activated experts to cut CPU↔GPU transfer during offloaded inference; finds shallow/deep-layer experts tolerate bounded error, mid-layer experts don't — evidence that compression intensity should vary by layer for streaming MoE — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.07727)
- KTransformers (SOSP '25): CPU/GPU hybrid MoE serving with AMX kernels + Expert Deferral; 1.66–19.74x speedups; experts moved whole, no basis/delta — [ACM](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3731569.3764843)
- HybriMoE (arXiv 2504.05897, Apr 2025): hybrid CPU-GPU scheduling + expert cache management on ktransformers; 1.33x/1.70x prefill/decode; whole-expert granularity — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.05897)
- DALI (arXiv 2602.03495, Feb 2026): workload-aware MoE offload on local PCs vs llama.cpp/ktransformers/MoE-Lightning/HybriMoE — current SOTA baselines are all whole-expert movers — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.03495v1)
- FluxMoE (arXiv 2604.02715, 2026 ASPLOS-track): "expert paging" — treats expert weights as transient streamed resources; keeps *compressed copies* GPU-resident + streams from host DRAM, overlapping layer i compute with layer i+1 prefetch; residency level alpha adapted online (48x4x7 experts offloaded, ~5.3 GB reclaimed) without throughput loss — the nearest "resident-compressed + streamed" architecture, but delta is a quantization copy, not a low-rank basis decomposition — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.02715v2)
- ATSInfer (arXiv 2607.10183, 2026): tensor-granularity CPU-GPU offloading on consumer devices; notes vLLM v0.18 does NOT support offloading for Qwen3-Next/GPT-OSS and ktransformers v0.5.2 lacks MXFP4 CPU kernels — engine support for Next-class models is thin — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.10183v2)
- MoE-Infinity (open source): activation-aware expert offload to host RAM + SSD; offloads 288 routed FP8 experts per layer for a Qwen3.5-MoE-class model with backbone/shared-expert resident — closest open engine to the PS5 pattern, whole-expert streaming — [GitHub](https://github.com/EfficientMoE/MoE-Infinity)
- Fiddler (ICLR 2025): CPU-gpu orchestration, offloads expert *computation* to CPU rather than streaming weights — orthogonal axis — [ICLR](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/8cd1ce03ea58b3d7dfd809e4d42f08ea-Paper-Conference.pdf)
- Energy caution: "SSD Offloading for LLM MoE Weights Considered Harmful in Energy Efficiency" (arXiv 2508.06978): SSD-read energy per bit ≫ DRAM; up to ~12x per-token energy vs HBM for DeepSeek-R1-class offload — a 4–8x traffic cut is the exact lever this paper says is needed to make Flash-streamed MoE sane — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.06978)
- SMoE (arXiv 2508.18983, v3 May 2026): edge MoE via expert *substitution* (swap low-score activated experts for functionally similar cached ones): 48% lower decode latency, 60%+ cache hit rate, "nearly lossless" — the merge-into-cached-expert cousin of delta streaming — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.18983)
- ktransformers/Strata/BlobPager/vLLM expert-offload docs: none mention basis/delta or low-rank residual expert streaming in the searched corpus (Strata itself is a KV-cache system, not expert streaming).

### Inferences
- Novelty verdict: "stream the delta, keep the basis resident" as a *system* is unpublished; arXiv 2512.17073 is the essential citation to position against (it streams per-expert rank-r *quantization* compensators; it does not extract a shared basis and does not claim basis+delta as the storage format). A PS5 implementation would be first for the basis+delta variant — the paper to cite as prior art is 2512.17073, and the delta must beat its accuracy-per-byte to claim novelty of benefit, not just mechanism.
- PS5 adaptation: PC-side packer = D2-MoE/SLBF-style fit (Fisher-merge or data-free reconstruction per layer), emit resident basis blob (all 48 layers' W_base, ~a few GB Q8 in RAM) + per-expert delta records (10–50 KB each) on SSD. Engine = expert-cache design from MoE-Lightning/HybriMoE lineage, but each miss streams a delta record, not a 3 MB expert. GPU kernel = resident-basis GEMV + batched delta correction (see Q5). Layer-sensitivity results from 2509.07727 suggest allocating higher delta ranks to middle layers and near-zero deltas in shallow/deep ones.

### Gaps
- Nobody has measured basis+delta recomposition end-to-end on an SSD-streamed MoE (tokens/s, bytes/token) — the delta-streaming claim is mechanistically supported (2512.17073 shows low-rank factors are cheap to stream and effective for *quantization* residuals) but quantitatively unproven for *full-rank* expert residuals.
- No published engine (llama.cpp, vLLM, SGLang, ktransformers) has a basis+delta expert code path.

## Q4. What compression ratios hold quality on 100B+ MoE, and interaction with quantization

### Takeaway
Direct quantization on 100B+ MoE: Q4-class GGUF costs ~0.5–1.4% on DeepSeek-R1/V3 weighted-average suites; Q3 costs ~1.4–1.9%; Q2 collapses (9–15%). Basis/delta reconstruction on 30B–122B Qwen3-class: within −0.6 avg-points at 24% total-parameter reduction (Qwen3-30B-A3B) and at-or-above baseline at 34% (Qwen3.5-122B-A10B), collapsing at 37% on Gemma4-26B (−3.3). For streaming purposes: basis at Q8 resident + delta streamed is already the published recipe in 2512.17073 (INT2/3 experts + INT3 quantized rank-r compensators), and MiLo shows compensators themselves can be INT3-quantized without losing effect. Codebook (QuIP#/QTIP-style) residual quantization of bases or deltas: no published MoE application found.

### Cited Findings
- DeepSeek quantization study (arXiv 2505.02390, May 2025): DeepSeek-V3 weighted-avg accuracy drop vs FP8: Q4_K_M −1.35%, Q3_K_M −1.85%, Q2_K_L −14.66%, DQ3_K_M (dynamic 3-bit) −0.30%; DeepSeek-R1: "Q4 exhibits minimal degradation vs FP8"; UD-Q2_K_XL (dynamic 2-bit) beats static Q3_K_M — dynamic per-layer bit allocation recovers most of the Q3 gap — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.02390v2)
- "An empirical study of Qwen3 quantization" (Visual Intelligence 4(1), 2026): Qwen3 holds competitive performance at moderate bit-widths (4–8 bit) but degrades notably on language tasks at ultra-low precision — [journal](https://research.buaa.edu.cn/en/publications/an-empirical-study-of-qwen3-quantization)
- "Is GPT-OSS Good?" (arXiv 2508.12461): gpt-oss-120B (native MXFP4) matches/beats gpt-oss-20B on ~half of benchmarks (inverse-scaling anomaly); MoE gpt-oss class is broadly competitive at 4-bit native — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.12461)
- MC-MoE (ICLR 2025, Mixtral): mixed-precision expert quantization + dynamic pruning: 2.54 bits avg, 76.6% of parameters compressed, 3.8% avg accuracy loss; MC# (arXiv 2510.10962, Oct 2025, DeepSeek-VL2 542B-class): 6.2x weight reduction at 2.57 avg bits, 1.7% accuracy loss on 5 multimodal benchmarks — [MC-MoE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.06270); [MC#](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.10962)
- Bandwidth paper (2512.17073) quantizes experts INT2/INT3 *and the compensators INT3* and still recovers accuracy (numbers in Q3) — direct evidence the streamed delta tolerates 3-bit coding — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.17073v1)
- MiLo (MLSys 2025): INT3 MoE + mixture of adaptive low-rank compensators (expert-side rank ~32, kurtosis-adaptive); compensators quantized to INT3 with no effectiveness loss; W3A16 fused kernel gives measured latency speedups; calibration-free — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.02658), [repo](https://github.com/Supercomputing-System-AI-Lab/MiLo)
- Kurtosis predicts quantization residual norm (both 2512.17073 and MiLo find the same correlation; cf. KurTail, arXiv 2503.01483) → per-expert delta rank/bit allocation can be computed offline with no calibration data — [2512.17073](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.17073v1); [MiLo](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.02658)
- SLBF quality-vs-ratio on Qwen3-class models: see Q1 numbers (−0.6 avg pts @24% on 30B-A3B; +1.7 @34% on Qwen3.5-122B-A10B; −3.3 @37% on Gemma4-26B) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.09342v1)
- Router-score skew evidence (2512.17073, Fig. 3): top-1 expert gets 0.41–0.60 softmax mass vs 0.17–0.22 for top-2 on Mixtral; DeepSeek-MoE is flatter → quality-per-byte of *uneven* precision restoration depends on routing entropy. A 512-expert top-10 model like Flash-Next likely behaves Mixtral-to-DeepSeek-like; budget compensation ranks by router score, not uniformly.

### Inferences
- Budget allocation implication: at equal total streamed bytes, "Q8 basis + Q4 (or INT3) delta" should dominate "Q4 experts streamed" because the basis is error-free for all 512 experts while each expert's streamed part only carries its own residual. 2512.17073's INT2+INT3-comp layout is the published proof-of-concept of the quantized-delta format.
- 1%/3%/10% agentic-degradation targets: proxying from the nearest measured curves — Q4-class quantization and ~25% total parameter reduction sit in the ≤1–1.5% zone; ~35–40% reduction and Q3-class sit near 2–3%; Q2/50%+ reductions blow past 10%. A 4–8x streamed-byte reduction (1.4 → 0.18–0.35 GiB/token) corresponds to expert *storage* compression of similar order — beyond the demonstrated ≤2.5x storage zone of this family, so holding ≤3% at 8x requires the resident basis to absorb most expert variance and has not been demonstrated for expert counts of 512.
- PS5 packer view: emit basis at Q8 (RAM-resident, read 48x/token is negligible), deltas at INT3–INT4 with kurtosis-graded ranks (r=0 for cold/low-kurtosis experts, up to r=32 for hot high-kurtosis ones), mirroring 2512.17073's bucket scheme; keep the shared expert and first/last MoE layers at full streamed precision per 2509.07727's layer-sensitivity finding.

### Gaps
- No published agentic (function-calling/multi-step tool-use) degradation curve for any basis/delta or low-bit MoE at 100B+ — all quality numbers are static benchmarks; agent-specific cliffs (DeepSeek paper notes math/GPQA *improved* at Q4 while other tasks dropped) remain unquantified for Flash-Next-class models.
- QuIP#/QTIP-style codebook quantization of shared bases or deltas: no MoE-specific paper found.
- No 512-expert granularity quality curve: SLBF's Qwen3-30B test is 128-expert top-8; extrapolation to 512 top-10 (each expert narrower, more redundant) is untested in either direction.

## Q5. Compute cost of applying low-rank deltas on an idle RDNA3 GPU; fused kernels

### Takeaway
Delta application is free at the streamed operating point by ~3–4 orders of magnitude: for Qwen3-Next-class shapes, rank-8 deltas across 480 expert-draws/token add ~60 MFLOPs/token (<0.2% of the PS5 GPU even at the 6–10 tok/s streamed rate), versus ~6 GB/token of resident+delta traffic that dominates time. Fused quantized+low-rank-correction kernels exist (MiLo's MLSys-2025 INT3 W3A16 GeMM with on-the-fly UV correction; the bandwidth paper reconstructs on-device) but nothing shipped in llama.cpp/vLLM/SGLang; the PS5 kernel would be new code, closest to MiLo's.

### Cited Findings
- MiLo (MLSys 2025): ships real fused W3A16 GeMM CUDA kernels that compute the quantized main term and apply the low-rank compensator with dequant/GeMM/pipeline overlap — "measured latency speedups"; built on Marlin/HQQ — [repo](https://github.com/Supercomputing-System-AI-Lab/MiLo), [paper](https://proceedings.mlsys.org/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/9032e5c9ec394ce768a2fa9bdc56af6c-Abstract-Conference.html)
- 2512.17073 implements on-device reconstruction W_hat = Q^-1(Q(W)) + U·V within an offload pipeline and reports net throughput gains despite the added compute — i.e., published confirmation that correction compute is dominated by saved transfer at I/O-bound operating points — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.17073v1)
- D2-MoE claims its decomposition is "structured and acceleratable" for inference but evaluates accuracy, not kernels — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.17298)
- Their roofline profile: in offloaded MoE inference, GPU expert compute is a small fraction of token time; the GPU idles while weights stream (their Figure 1) — the same regime as PS5's ~95% idle GPU — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.17073v1)

### Inferences
- FLOPs arithmetic (my computation, Qwen3-Next-class shapes d=2048, f=512): per expert draw, 3 projections, rank r: 3 · 2r(n+m) ≈ 30.7k·r FLOPs; per token: ×48 layers ×10 draws = 14.7·r MFLOP. r=8 → 0.118 GFLOP/token; at 10 tok/s = 1.2 GFLOP/s = 0.03% of 3.9 TFLOPS. Even r=64 → 4.7 GFLOP/s = 0.12%. The main GEMV itself (Q8 basis, 480×1.05M MACs) is only ~1 GFLOP/token, so total compute stays <1% of GPU peak; the token time is, and remains, bandwidth-bound. Conclusion: delta application is compute-free at any sane rank; design entirely for bytes, not FLOPs.
- Bytes arithmetic: rank-r fp16 delta = 5.1·r KB per projection; per token = 1440·r KB. r=8 → 11.8 MB/token?? — careful: streamed draws are 480 (48 layers × 10), not 1440; ×3 projections ×5.1r KB = 7.3·r MB/token → r=8: 59 MB/token (0.06 GiB), r=32: 236 MB/token, r=64: 472 MB/token. So the 0.2–0.4 GiB target corresponds to fp16 deltas of rank ~27–55 (or fp8/int3 at higher rank). Q8 deltas halve this; INT3-compensator encoding (per 2512.17073) halves again.
- PS5 GPU kernel: one resident-basis GEMV per layer (shared for all 10 draws of that layer) + 10 batched skinny delta GEMVs; on RDNA3 express the delta batch as one tall tall-skinny GEMM (M=10 tokens×? — decode is M=1, so it is 30 dependent GEMVs of 2560×r per layer, latency-bound but hidden under SSD prefetch of the *next* layer's deltas). No published kernel does exactly this; MiLo's W3A16 + correction kernel is the template.

### Gaps
- No published fused basis+delta MoE kernel in llama.cpp/vLLM/SGLang/KTransformers; no RDNA-specific numbers.
- No measured wall-clock of rank-r correction under PCIe/Flash-streamed decode on console-class hardware.

## Q6. Router fidelity: does basis+delta recomposition shift top-10 routing?

### Takeaway
For weight-reconstruction methods (D2-MoE, MoBE, SLBF, LightMoE, ResMoE, Sub-MoE) the router weights and expert count are untouched, and routing decisions are functions of the hidden state and W_g only — so each layer's *first* routing decision is bit-identical; no re-running of routing on reconstructed experts is needed. Top-k sets only drift *behaviorally*, because recomposed experts perturb hidden states fed to deeper routers. SLBF measured this directly: at 25% compression its per-layer MoE-output drift is lower than REAP (pruning) or HC-SMoE (merging) on Qwen3-30B (128 experts) — and argues pruning/merging have irreducible *structural* error (routing ties they can't escape) that weight reconstruction does not.

### Cited Findings
- SLBF structural analysis: pruning and merging induce "non-vanishing errors tied to routing and expert heterogeneity" (error ∝ router variability Var[r(x)] and functional gap ‖f_i−f_j‖²); weight reconstruction's error is bounded purely by the router-weighted parameter residual Σ_k g_k·E‖x‖²·‖W_k−Ŵ_k‖²_F, "preserving both expert structure and routing" — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.09342v1)
- SLBF Fig. 1b (measured): relative per-layer MoE-block output drift ‖δh‖/‖h‖ at 25% compression on Qwen3-30B-A3B: SLBF below REAP and HC-SMoE at every layer; structural costs of pruning/merging grow 3–4 orders of magnitude with depth — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.09342v1)
- 2512.17073 runs routing normally and *then* compensates streamed experts — router never observes reconstructed weights; their INT2 results (near-recovered MMLU) imply downstream routing stayed close enough to baseline — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.17073v1)
- Residual-sparsification paper's critique of routing-aware compression (dimension-level selection should be routing-aware; coarse expert-level bias mis-ranks dimensions across experts) — shows the family is aware of router-interaction effects — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.00575)

### Inferences
- PS5 answer: keep the router (and its Q8 weights) exactly as shipped; route on raw hidden states; apply basis+delta after selection. Routing fidelity degrades only second-order, and the *cheapest* way to keep it is to fit deltas to minimize ‖W_e − Ŵ_e‖ (all these methods' objective), not to re-run routing.
- Secondary caveat: with 512 narrow experts and strong specialization at depth (per SLBF Fig. 1a), deep-layer deltas need the most precision to avoid behavioral routing drift in later layers — matches 2509.07727's "middle layers most sensitive" finding.

### Gaps
- Nobody reports "top-k overlap fraction after basis+delta recomposition" directly (the SLBF drift curves are the closest measurement). No Flash-Next-class router study exists.
